Our read on the assignment

Hollywood has been a difficult leasing market, and El Centro reflects that.

Tours convert slowly and restaurant activity has been light, including on our own Hollywood assignments. Submarket vacancy sits at 8.0%, nearly triple its 2019 low, the past twelve months gave back 18,700 SF, and asking rents fell 1.9%. El Centro's ground floor has sat empty since the building opened, which is what a difficult market and an unfinished space tend to produce together.

That shapes how we would approach it. Progress here depends less on the scale of the marketing campaign than on three things: ownership's capital landing where operators need it, rents and improvement allowances set against what is actually transacting, and sustained direct contact with a focused list of operators.

We would concentrate that effort on food and beverage. It is where the demand that does exist is most consistent, it supports the strongest rents, and Amoeba together with 507 apartments overhead offers an operator something much of Hollywood cannot: a built-in weekday customer base.

How we would approach it
Concentrate on food and beverage. The category with the most consistent demand, and the one best able to draw traffic to the remaining suites.
Price against what is transacting. Rents and improvement allowances benchmarked to signed deals rather than the pro forma.
Make the space operator-ready. Elevated floors, demising walls, and vanilla-shell infrastructure largely determine which operators can bid at all.
Work a focused list consistently. A short roster of well-matched operators contacted monthly, rather than broad distribution.

Market knowledge

Hollywood retail vacancy stands at 8.0%, nearly triple its 2019 low, and asking rents fell 1.9% over the past year.

Demand is the constraint, not supply. The submarket gave back 18,700 SF over the past twelve months, and rent growth of −1.9% is the weakest since 2011, trailing the metro average. International tourism remains below pre-pandemic levels, while rising rents, wages and insurance have pushed some operators to forgo renewals.

Supply is the durable counterweight. Over the past decade the submarket's retail footprint contracted by 210,000 SF as demolitions outpaced development, while 6,643 residential units were added — a 79% increase. Nothing is in the construction pipeline, and limited land plus Hollywood's zoning and entitlement requirements make that unlikely to reverse.

More residents, fewer storefronts, no pipeline. A 29,000 SF ground floor on the Walk of Fame is not competing with new construction, and the $750 million Film and Tax Credit Program and LA28 should help demand recover. The scarcity is genuine; the challenge is execution.

Leasing strategy

The recommendation

Marketed as one place, not thirteen suites.

El Centro's ground floor should go to market as a single food-and-beverage-led collection with Amoeba as its cultural anchor. Thirteen separate listings tend to compete with one another for the same small-format tenant. Presented as a single collection, the ground floor reads differently to operators and supports a clearer merchandising decision.

We are deliberately not committing to a fixed plan for how that happens. Demising, use mix and the approval path get built around the operators we can actually sign — and in this market, flexibility is worth more than a schedule drawn before the tenants existed.
